Research Interests

My research interests lie broadly in stochastic control, nonlinear filtering, and partially observed Markov decision processes (POMDPs). I am particularly interested in the robustness, stability, and approximation of control and filtering systems under partial information.

In discrete-time settings, I have studied the impact of model mismatch and quantization on the stability and convergence of belief-state dynamics, deriving explicit bounds on value functions and identifying conditions for near-optimality. In particular, I have investigated the Wasserstein and total-variation continuity of filter kernels under model perturbations, robustness in the average-cost setting, and unique ergodicity results for control-free models.

More recently, I have been focusing on continuous-time stochastic systems under partial observation, especially diffusion processes — studying robustness to prior mismatch and how discrete-time approximations via time and observation quantization can be used to analyze and control such systems.
